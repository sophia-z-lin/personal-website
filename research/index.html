Research.

Evaluation validity, uncertainty, and evidence that can be inspected.

I work on the reliability of AI systems and the methods used to judge them. Across language-model evaluation and clinical prediction, I am interested in what a measurement captures, what it misses, and which conclusions it can support.

Projects

NIST2026–present

Characterizing LLM Judges as Measurement Instruments

I led the design of a measurement framework testing entailment, claim reasoning and decomposition, error recognition, confidence, and abstention. More than 15 probes compare blind, correct-guidance, and deliberately false-guidance conditions to separate evaluation capability from prompt sensitivity and sycophancy.

Project lead
NISTOngoing

Adversarial Verification for Agentic AI Grounding

I am designing controlled experiments to test whether independent verifier agents with competing objectives improve factual grounding. The evaluation tooling tests citation-grounded reasoning, deception, hallucination, contradiction handling, and reliance on retrieved evidence when it conflicts with parametric knowledge.

Project lead
RSI · MIT CSAIL · Harvard Medical School2024

Bayesian Modeling of Multi-Stage Censoring

I co-developed a Bayesian model of sequential selection and censoring to recover latent patient risk from incomplete clinical decision pipelines and examine disparities in triage.

Stony Brook University2023–2024

FRISTS: Interpretable Time-Series Prediction

I led development of an interpretable model that extracts temporal patterns from clinical records and produces human-auditable risk estimates. We developed and validated the method using 18 million longitudinal health records, outperforming prior heart-failure prediction baselines.

Longitudinal Analysis of Federal ROTC Programs

I built Python, R, and SAS pipelines integrating 30 years of enrollment, commissioning, funding, and program data, with edit checks and clustering analyses across inconsistent longitudinal records.
