Sophia Lin.

I am a sophomore at Harvard studying computer science, statistics, and philosophy, and a research associate at NIST working on adversarial evaluation and measurement for agentic AI.

My main research interest is evals: measuring what AI systems can do, where they fail, and whether we can reliably oversee them. At NIST, I study whether independent, competing-objective agents can expose unsupported or incomplete claims in other agents’ outputs. More broadly, I am interested in preserving meaningful human control as AI systems gain capability, access, and autonomy. I care about risks ranging from present-day misuse and unreliable deployment to longer-run loss of control, particularly where effective human oversight is difficult.

Previously, I developed a Bayesian model of multi-stage censoring with Shuvom Sadhuka, Bonnie Berger, and Emma Pierson. The model jointly represents sequential human decisions and selectively observed outcomes; we applied it to emergency-care data to study gender disparities in hospital and ICU admission. I also developed an interpretable disease-prediction method with Xinyu Dong and Fusheng Wang that predicted heart failure from longitudinal health records while identifying the clinical history driving its predictions.

Outside research, I’m a classical pianist and an avid X-Men comics fan. I also write on a smorgasbord of personal, global, and technical topics.
